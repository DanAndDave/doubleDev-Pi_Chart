# Memory benchmark fact planting

Type: research
Status: closed
Blocked by: —

## Question

How do long-conversation memory benchmarks plant facts and score retention? Examples include LongMemEval, LoCoMo, MemoryAgentBench, and needle-in-a-haystack variants for agents. For each, report:

- the distances used;
- the fact taxonomy, including updates, contradictions, temporal facts and abstention;
- how distractors are built;
- whether probes are asked directly or needed implicitly by a later task;
- the scoring method (exact match or a judge);
- how the benchmark separates a retrieval failure from a reasoning failure.

## Resolution

Nearly every benchmark plants answer-bearing evidence (a "needle") in filler (a "haystack") and then asks about it. They differ along four axes. **Distance** is measured in tokens or depth-percent for the NIAH family (NIAH, RULER, NoLiMa, MRCR, HaystackCraft, BABILong). For conversation-memory benchmarks it is measured in sessions, conversations or weeks (MSC, LoCoMo, LongMemEval, ConvoMem, BEAM, Ground Truth First). None of the surveyed benchmarks indexes distance by agent Turns, and only one (Ground Truth First) asks whether the evidence was *evicted* from a budgeted view at probe time. **Taxonomies** have converged on LongMemEval's five abilities: extraction, multi-session, temporal, knowledge update and abstention. Newer benchmarks add contradiction resolution (BEAM), *implicit* invalidation (STALE), false-premise rejection (LongMemEval ABS, LME-V2, STALE) and expired-state questions (Ground Truth First). **Distractors** started as irrelevant filler (Paul Graham essays, PG19, ShareGPT). They became same-distribution near-misses (RULER multi-key, MRCR, the old values in FactConsolidation) and then retriever-built or self-generated distractors (HaystackCraft). Two findings complicate the picture: filler that is *stylistically different* from the evidence lets models shortcut (ConvoMem's critique of LongMemEval), and lexical overlap between question and needle inflates recall (NoLiMa). **Probes** are almost always direct questions. Implicit probes, where a later task silently needs the fact, appear only in PrefEval, BEAM's instruction and preference following, STALE's policy-adaptation probe and MemoryArena's interdependent subtasks. **Scoring** splits by answer form. Synthetic tasks use exact, substring or similarity match: RULER, BABILong, NoLiMa, MRCR, MemoryAgentBench SubEM, and v2 of the original NIAH harness. Free-form answers use an LLM judge validated against humans: LongMemEval reports 97–98% agreement and STALE 95.8%. LoCoMo uses token F1. A non-peer-reviewed audit, cited by Ground Truth First, reports that a LoCoMo judge accepted up to 63% of deliberately wrong answers. **Retrieval and reasoning failures are separated** by five mechanisms. Most benchmarks use only one or two of them:

1. An **oracle or short-context control**: the same question with only the evidence. Examples are LongMemEval Oracle, Chroma's "focused" prompts, NoLiMa's base score at 250–1K tokens, BABILong at 0K, MemoryAgentBench at 6K, and ConvoMem's "positive validation".
2. **Evidence-location labels plus Recall@k** on what the memory returned. Examples are LongMemEval, LoCoMo turn IDs and AMA-Bench `turn_id`s. LongMemEval uses these labels to split errors into retrieval-correct/answer-wrong quadrants.
3. A **no-evidence control** that catches parametric guessing (LME-V2's no-history baseline, ConvoMem's "negative validation", Ground Truth First's no-memory control).
4. A **fixed reader** behind the memory under test (LME-V2's context-gathering API).
5. **Error taxonomies or multi-probe decompositions** on one planted fact (PrefEval's four error types; STALE's SR/PR/IPA probes).

| **LongMemEval** (Wu et al. 2024) | S: ~115K tokens (~50 sessions). M: 500 sessions, ~1.5M tokens. Oracle: evidence sessions only. | **LongMemEval-V2** (Wu et al. 2026) | Up to 500 web-agent trajectories, 115M tokens | Static state recall, dynamic state tracking, workflow knowledge, environment gotchas, premise awareness (abstention) | Non-answer-bearing trajectories in the same environment | Direct | Deterministic matchers for structured answers; LLM judge for gotchas and abstention | **No-history baseline** (parametric), **oracle** trajectories, and a **fixed reader** over memory output truncated to 200K, which isolates memory from reading |
| **STALE** (2026) | Up to 150K tokens; 400 scenarios, 1,200 queries | **Implicit conflict**: a later observation invalidates an earlier belief without negating it, including cascading invalidation of a related attribute | LongMemEval sessions, filtered so that nothing else updates the target state | One direct (state resolution), one false-premise (premise resistance), one **implicit** (policy adaptation) probe per scenario | LLM judge, 95.8% agreement with humans, conservative | The three probes on the same plant separate "detected the update" from "acted on it". The authors concede some entanglement with long-context retrieval remains |
| **Ground Truth First** (2026) | 3-week and 9-week horizons, checkpoints at weeks 3, 6 and 9; "early-chapter" probes aimed at the oldest material | Facts planted *before* the text is rendered, each with a validity interval and volatility class; 15 types incl. knowledge update (current and past), expired state, conflict resolution, abstention, false premise, injection probes | The rest of a synthetic, fictional life across chat and email channels | Direct, as-of-date question sets | Versioned LLM judge, cross-family re-judging, 3 replicates, exact sign-flip tests | A **fidelity verifier** confirms each plant. Controls: no-memory and full-history. Write-stage quality is scored per fact. Loss is attributed to *eviction*: the budgeted map fell from 96% to 72% on early probes while full history held 160/162 |

### Implications for pi-chart

Options and trade-offs only. These feed ticket 07 (planted-fact matrix).

- **Unit of distance.** One option is Turns between plant and probe, with each probe also tagged by where the fact was at probe time: Opening, Pin, tail, or evicted. Ground Truth First shows eviction, not raw length, drives the loss in budgeted memory. Turn distance alone conflates Budget settings with distance. Recording the location state needs Journal access but is directly observable for pi-chart.
- **Oracle control (plant + probe only).** A short-context arm per probe, as in NoLiMa's base score and LongMemEval Oracle, separates "the model can't use the fact" from "the window lost it". It costs extra runs but is cheap if the probe Turn is replayable (ticket 04).
- **No-plant control.** Running without the plant, or with fictional values (the current codeword already qualifies), catches guessing. LME-V2, ConvoMem and Ground Truth First all report parametric leakage as a real confound. It roughly doubles probe runs unless sampled.
- **Recall@k from the Journal.** `recall_earlier_turns` pulls are logged, so each probe can be classed in LongMemEval's quadrants: fact not pulled / pulled but answered wrong / pulled and right / right without a pull (Opening, tail, or guess). This matches ticket 07's attribution need. It needs the plant's Turn ID recorded and a match rule for pulled content.
- **Fact types mapped to precedents.** Literal token → NIAH/IE. Decision with reason → multi-hop (LoCoMo, FactConsolidation-MH). Constraint on later behaviour → PrefEval, BEAM instruction following, STALE policy adaptation. Superseded fact → knowledge update, FactConsolidation, STALE. Adding false-premise and abstention probes (LongMemEval ABS, LME-V2, STALE premise resistance) would penalise recall that returns a stale or nonexistent value; leaving them out keeps the matrix smaller.
- **Distractors.** Same-distribution near-misses (earlier build numbers, an old value of a superseded fact) follow the MRCR, RULER multi-key and FactConsolidation precedent and stress ordering. Irrelevant filler is cheaper but nearly saturated. Filler stylistically unlike the plant risks ConvoMem's shortcut critique.
- **Lexical overlap.** `recall_earlier_turns` is a search tool. Probes that reuse the plant's wording overstate recall (NoLiMa). A paired low-overlap variant measures the harder case but needs hand-written associations.
- **Direct vs implicit probe.** A direct probe with tools forbidden (the current `bench-tokens` probe) measures the window alone. A direct probe with tools allowed measures window plus recall. An implicit, task-embedded probe (PrefEval, MemoryArena) measures what the user cares about but, as AMA-Bench notes, mixes memory errors with policy errors. Running a direct and an implicit probe on the same plant, as STALE does, makes the gap visible at the cost of more judge calls.
- **Scoring.** Substring or exact match suits literal tokens and is judge-free; constraint and implicit probes need a judge. If a judge is used, precedent is to version it, validate it against human labels, and re-judge with a second model family (LongMemEval, STALE, Ground Truth First), applying it identically to both arms. LoCoMo's reported judge leniency is the counter-example.
- **Sample size.** RULER uses 500 examples per cell; ConvoMem argues small sets lack statistical power; Ground Truth First pairs per-user effects with exact sign-flip tests. This bears on ticket 13.
